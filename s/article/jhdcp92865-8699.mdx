## How Redaction Protects Your Data

Some of the most powerful features in your Invoca account — such as Signal AI — use call recordings and transcripts to give you actionable insights based on the real words and phrases spoken during your calls. These features work best when as much of the conversation as possible is available for analysis. At the same time, not all information should live in your account: sensitive personal and financial details that callers share, often incidentally, carry real risk if they're stored, and they aren't necessary for generating business insights.

To help protect sensitive data, Invoca automatically applies Standard Redaction to both call recordings and transcripts. Redaction happens as part of the recording pipeline itself, rather than as a cleanup step after the fact, so sensitive values are identified and removed before storage rather than found and deleted afterward. This ensures personal and financial information is never stored in your account, supporting your compliance efforts and safeguarding caller privacy, while still preserving the surrounding conversation for downstream features to use.

In addition, you can configure your account to automatically delete call recordings after a set retention period of your choice. This gives you a second, independent layer of control over how long call data persists — redaction removes specific sensitive fields at the time of recording, while retention settings govern how long the remaining (already-redacted) recording and transcript are kept before being purged entirely.

Beginning May 5th, Invoca's Standard Redaction functionality will be enabled across all networks, except those having purchased the Advanced Redaction feature. This means most customers do not need to take any action to benefit from this protection — it is applied automatically at the network level rather than requiring per-user or per-campaign configuration. Networks that have purchased Advanced Redaction will continue to use that feature's configuration instead, since Advanced Redaction offers a superset of Standard Redaction's capabilities.

Standard redaction automatically removes sensitive information such as credit card numbers, expiration dates, CVV codes, Social Security numbers, and passwords from the audio recording and transcription of recorded calls. This covers the categories of data most commonly spoken aloud during a call — for example, a caller reading off a card number to complete a payment, or confirming their identity with a Social Security number — and removes them from both the audio track and any corresponding transcript text, so the sensitive value cannot be reconstructed from either source.

## How Redaction Works

When a call is recorded in Invoca, sensitive data is automatically redacted from both the audio recording and the transcript (if transcription is enabled) before any data is stored or processed further.

With redaction enabled, Invoca’s AI detects and removes sensitive words, phrases, and numbers in memory, in real time before any data is written to disk. This means:

- Unredacted audio and text never touch Invoca servers or our transcription partners.
- Redaction is applied consistently to both recordings and transcripts.

**Fields Included in Standard Redaction:**

- **PII Data:** DOB, Driver's License, SSN, Passport #, and Passwords
- **PCI Data:** Credit Card #, Exp Date, CCV, Bank Acct #, and Routing #

<Note>
**Note**

This Standard Redaction level is being made mandatory for all new customers and will be deployed across all networks. At the moment, redaction cannot be disabled or customized to capture partial entities or additional data types.
</Note>

## Understanding PCI DSS and Invoca’s Role in Protecting Your Data

The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) is a global standard designed to protect credit card information and prevent fraud. If a company handles payment and cardholder data—whether it's storing, processing, or transmitting this information—they must follow PCI DSS rules. This applies to major credit card brands like Visa, MasterCard, American Express, Discover, and JCB.  

## Invoca's PCI Certification

Invoca is PCI certified, meaning we meet and adhere to the strict security standards required by PCI DSS. Every year, an approved auditor checks our security systems to ensure we’re protecting customer data at all levels, from our infrastructure to our services.

#### This certification covers all aspects of credit card processing, including phone calls where customers may share their payment details. If these calls are recorded or transcribed, the system must be PCI certified; otherwise, neither the system nor the businesses using it are compliant with PCI DSS.
